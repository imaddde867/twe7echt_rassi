# ES pilot, first run on Roihu (jobs 2004461 and 2004551)

Provenance. Both jobs: `sbatch -A project_2020845 slurm/train_es.sbatch N` on the `small` partition, 32 workers on one
x86_64 node, repository at commit 1547e4e (its tree is identical to `main` at 7902d3d, checked with `git diff`),
module `python-data` 3.12. Training setup is the script's: 32 antithetic pairs, 2 seeds per evaluation, 20 agents,
20 years, hidden layers (32, 32), reward `w_survival=1.0, w_wealth=0.5, w_health=0.25, cash_scale=20000`.
Held-out evaluation: 20 seeds (9000000 to 9000019), 40 agents, 30 years, the script's `lifesim.rl.evaluate` call.

- **2004461** (`train_es.sbatch 20`): behaviour cloning, then a 20-generation ES run. COMPLETED, 13 min 39 s elapsed,
  26 208 CPU-seconds (about 7.3 CPU-hours). The network evaluated below is its `best.npz`, see "Which network".
- **2004551** (`train_es.sbatch 1`): the control. With one generation, generation 0 scores and saves the
  behaviour-cloned network before any ES update, so its `best.npz` is the network ES started from (zero updates).
  COMPLETED, 3 min 11 s elapsed. Its cloning loss (0.334 to 0.297) and generation-0 fitness (1.608) equal the
  pilot's, and its `rule` and `random` rows equal the pilot's, so both used the same cloned network and the same
  held-out seeds.

## Which network is "ES" in the tables

`es.py` scores the current weights, saves them to `best.npz` if they beat the best so far, and only then applies the
ES update. So the weights scored at generation g have had g updates. The pilot's best noisy fitness was at
generation 11, so the evaluated network is the **best checkpoint of the 20-generation run, selected at generation 11:
the weights after 11 updates**, not the weights after all 20. `latest.npz` (after generation 19) was evaluated
later and scores the same (reward 1.690 vs 1.689, see "Reward components").
Selecting the maximum of 20 noisy 2-seed scores also favours a lucky checkpoint; the held-out seeds are not the
ones it was selected on, which is why the held-out table is the evidence.

`theta_fitness` for the current weights, on 2 fresh seeds each generation: 1.608 at generation 0, then between

## What this shows

1. The pipeline runs end to end on Roihu, for the first time for the ES script: cloning 127 to 133 s, about 33 s per
   generation, evaluation included.
2. For training seed 0, behaviour cloning alone already beats `rule` on this reward (1.613 vs 1.515), and the best ES
   checkpoint beats the clone (1.689 vs 1.613). The clone's lead does not generalise: the seed-1 clone scores 1.410,
   below `rule` (`docs/results/es_replication_2004980.md`).
3. Paired by held-out seed (the 20 seeds are the same worlds for every policy; `paired_test.py` on the two eval
   files): ES checkpoint minus clone +0.0755 (se 0.0041, t = 18.3, df 19), higher on 20 of 20 seeds; clone minus
   rule +0.0981 (se 0.0094, t = 10.5), 20 of 20; ES checkpoint minus rule +0.1736 (se 0.0089, t = 19.4), 20 of 20.
   Critical value 2.093 for one test; 2.625 with a Bonferroni correction for three two-sided tests at 0.05 (checked
   with `scipy.stats.t.ppf`). All three pass by a wide margin.
4. The reward's cash term is `0.5 * tanh(cash / 20000)` (`lifesim/rl/reward.py`), which is already at about its
   ceiling for cash above roughly 60k, so median cash (111k, 113k, 270k) cannot explain the reward differences. The
   three reward terms were then measured directly (next section): both gaps are almost entirely the wealth term. A
   split I did earlier from the evaluator's `mean_health` was not valid (that column averages every agent, but the
   reward counts health only for living agents, and an agent that dies of old age keeps its health value in the
   outcome table, `world.py`, `_die`); it overstated the health term by about 0.02.
5. The clone treats more illnesses than `rule` (0.845 vs 0.633). It is a candidate explanation for part of the first
   gain; it was not tested, and the reward has no illness term.

## Reward components, measured (code at 3caf6b7)

`lifesim.rl.evaluate` writes the three weighted terms of the reward, averaged over agents per seed (PR #10). Mean
over the same 20 held-out seeds, standard deviation across seeds in parentheses:

| policy | survival | wealth | health | sum | mean of `tanh(cash / 20000)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule | 0.970 (0.022) | 0.323 (0.037) | 0.223 (0.014) | 1.516 | 0.646 |
| clone, no ES updates | 0.969 (0.021) | 0.423 (0.017) | 0.221 (0.013) | 1.613 | 0.846 |
| ES best checkpoint (11 updates) | 0.970 (0.022) | 0.493 (0.009) | 0.226 (0.013) | 1.689 | 0.986 |
| ES `latest.npz` (20 updates) | 0.970 (0.022) | 0.494 (0.009) | 0.226 (0.013) | 1.690 | 0.988 |

The sums match the printed rewards (1.515, 1.613, 1.689, 1.690) up to rounding. Differences between rows:

- Clone minus rule, +0.098: wealth +0.100, survival -0.001, health -0.002.
- ES best minus clone, +0.0755: wealth +0.070, health +0.005, survival +0.001.
- `latest.npz` minus best, +0.001: the means are indistinguishable (no paired test was run on that pair), so the
  extra nine updates changed nothing visible here.

The wealth term has a ceiling of 0.5. The clone is 0.077 below it and ES within 0.007, which is the low-cash tail
the median hides: the ES network closes about 91% of the clone's gap to the ceiling. How many agents are in that
tail, and why the clone ends up there, was not measured (no per-agent data).

from Slurm job 2004893 (`--policies rule`). The `rule` job printed `module: command not found` (the module was not
loaded in that shell) and still reproduced the earlier `rule` row exactly. The networks' rows reproduce the earlier

## What this does not support

- One training seed in this pilot (seed 1 is in `docs/results/es_replication_2004980.md`) and one reward weighting.
  The reward is an assumption; `w_wealth` and `cash_scale` both decide what is rewarded.
- Eleven updates, not twenty, behind the evaluated ES network; and 20 of the planned 300 generations. Nothing here
  says where training ends up.
- The paired tests are over 20 held-out worlds for one trained network per row. They say these networks differ on
  these worlds; they do not say a second training run (another `--seed`) would land in the same place.
- No statement about the cash distribution of individual agents (the low-cash tail is inferred from the means, not
  counted), or about why the clone ends up with less cash than the ES network, or why it beats `rule`.
- The same model, defaults and reward as training: nothing here says the policy does well in a different world or
  for real people. The evaluation is a fixed population without births; the generations experiment is separate.
