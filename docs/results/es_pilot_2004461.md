# ES pilot, first run on Roihu (jobs 2004461 and 2004551)

Provenance. Both jobs: `sbatch -A project_2020845 slurm/train_es.sbatch N` on the `small` partition, 32 workers on one
x86_64 node, repository at commit 1547e4e (its tree is identical to `main` at 7902d3d, checked with `git diff`),
module `python-data` 3.12. Training setup is the script's: 32 antithetic pairs, 2 seeds per evaluation, 20 agents,
20 years, hidden layers (32, 32), reward `w_survival=1.0, w_wealth=0.5, w_health=0.25, cash_scale=20000`.
Held-out evaluation: 20 seeds, 40 agents, 30 years, the script's `lifesim.rl.evaluate` call.

- **2004461** (`train_es.sbatch 20`): behaviour cloning, then 20 ES generations. COMPLETED, 13 min 39 s elapsed,
  26 208 CPU-seconds (about 7.3 CPU-hours).
- **2004551** (`train_es.sbatch 1`): the control. With one generation, generation 0 scores and saves the
  behaviour-cloned network before any ES update, so its `best.npz` is the network ES started from. COMPLETED, 3 min
  11 s elapsed. Its cloning loss (0.334 to 0.297) and generation-0 fitness (1.608) equal the pilot's, and its `rule`
  and `random` rows equal the pilot's, so both used the same cloned network and the same held-out seeds.

The numbers were transcribed by hand from the terminal output of each job, rounded as printed. The originals are in
`~/lifesim/runs/rl_2004461/` and `~/lifesim/runs/rl_2004551/` on Roihu (git-ignored; `log.csv`, `eval.csv`,

## Training curve (job 2004461, `log.csv` as printed)

`theta_fitness` for the current weights, on 2 fresh seeds each generation: 1.608 at generation 0, then between
1.538 (generation 7) and 1.750 (generation 11), 1.669 at generation 19. There is no clean upward trend inside 20

## What this shows

1. The pipeline runs end to end on Roihu, for the first time for the ES script: cloning 127 to 133 s, about 33 s per
   generation, evaluation included.
2. Behaviour cloning alone already beats `rule` on this reward (1.613 vs 1.515). Years lived and health are equal
   within their spread, so the whole gain is in the wealth term of the reward, `0.5 * tanh(cash / 20000)`
   (`lifesim/rl/reward.py`). Splitting each mean reward as years/horizon + 0.25 * health/100 + the remainder, from
   the rounded table values: the first two terms sum to about 1.215 (rule), 1.212 (clone), 1.218 (clone + ES);
   the wealth term is about 0.30, 0.40 and 0.47. (Check: the random row gives 0.801/30 + 0.5 * tanh(-2011/20000)
   = -0.023, as printed.)
3. Twenty ES generations on top of that raise reward further (1.689 vs 1.613), again only through the wealth term.
   That term saturates: any agent with cash above roughly 60k already earns its 0.5 ceiling, so median cash (111k,
   113k, 270k) says nothing about the reward. The gain is fewer agents with little or negative cash, a lower tail
   that the table does not show; the per-agent cash distribution was not looked at.
5. Paired by held-out seed (the 20 seeds are the same worlds for every policy; reward from the two `eval.csv`
   files, copied by hand into a scratch script, whose means reproduce the table): ES minus clone +0.0755 (se
   0.0041, t = 18.3, df 19, critical 2.093), ES higher on 20 of 20 seeds; clone minus rule +0.0981 (se 0.0094,
   t = 10.5), 20 of 20; ES minus rule +0.1736 (se 0.0089, t = 19.4), 20 of 20. Three tests; with a Bonferroni
   correction the critical value is about 2.5, which all three pass by a wide margin. The unpaired figure I first
   gave (about 3 standard errors) was far too conservative.
4. The clone treats more illnesses than `rule` (0.845 vs 0.633). That is a candidate reason for fewer low-cash
   agents; it was not tested. The reward has no illness term.

## What this does not support

- One training run (one `--seed`), one reward weighting. The wealth term (`w_wealth=0.5`, `cash_scale=20000`) is all
  of both gains; a different weighting or scale could change or remove them. The reward is an assumption.
- The split above uses rounded table values, so each term is good to about two decimals at best. The training
  results come from `mean_health` and years as the evaluator prints them, which I assumed average the same
  per-agent quantities the reward does.
- Twenty of the planned 300 generations. It says nothing about where training ends up.
- Held-out seeds and a longer horizon (30 years, 40 agents) than training (20 years, 20 agents), but the same
  model, defaults and reward. Nothing here says the policy does well on a different world or on real people.
- The paired tests are over 20 held-out worlds for one trained network per row. They say these networks differ
  on these worlds; they do not say a second training run (another `--seed`) would land in the same place. That
  needs a replication, and the script cannot pass `--seed` yet.
- The evaluation compares policies in a fixed population without births; the generations experiment is separate.
